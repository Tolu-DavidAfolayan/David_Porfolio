Project Overview

This comprehensive Titanic survival prediction project leveraged machine learning to analyze the tragic maritime disaster and identify patterns that determined passenger survival. Using the famous Kaggle Titanic dataset with 891 training records and 418 test cases, I developed multiple classification models to predict survival outcomes based on passenger characteristics.

The analysis combined historical data science with modern machine learning techniques, featuring advanced feature engineering, multiple algorithm comparison, and statistical correlation analysis. Through careful data preprocessing and model optimization, the project achieved strong predictive performance while uncovering the social and economic factors that influenced survival during this historic tragedy.

The Challenge

The Titanic dataset presents classic machine learning challenges:

  • Missing Data: Significant gaps in Age (20%), Cabin (77%), and Embarked (0.2%) columns.
  • Class Imbalance: 61.6% fatalities vs 38.4% survivors creating prediction bias.
  • Feature Engineering: Extracting meaningful patterns from passenger names, tickets, and family relationships.
  • Categorical Encoding: Converting text data (Sex, Embarked) into numerical formats for ML algorithms.
  • Historical Context: Understanding how social class, gender, and age influenced survival in 1912.
  • Model Selection: Comparing multiple algorithms to find optimal performance for binary classification.

The Approach & Solution

I implemented a comprehensive classification pipeline with advanced preprocessing:

1. Data Wrangling & Feature Engineering

  • Missing Value Imputation: Age filled with median, Embarked with mode, Fare with median.
  • Feature Creation: Engineered FamilySize (SibSp + Parch) for family survival analysis.
  • Categorical Encoding: Mapped Sex (Female=0, Male=1) and Embarked (C=0, Q=1, S=2).
  • Data Transformation: Log transformation of Fare to handle skewed distribution.

2. Multi-Algorithm Machine Learning

  • Random Forest: Ensemble method with 500 estimators achieving best performance (83.2%).
  • Logistic Regression: Linear baseline model for comparison (80.2% accuracy).
  • Decision Tree: Interpretable single-tree model (80.2% accuracy).
  • Model Optimization: Hyperparameter tuning with max_depth=10 for Random Forest.

Historical Insights Discovered

  • Gender Bias: Women were 3.9x more likely to survive than men due to "women and children first" protocol.
  • Class Discrimination: First-class passengers were 2.6x more likely to survive than third-class.
  • Age Protection: Children and elderly showed different survival patterns reflecting evacuation priorities.
  • Economic Influence: Higher ticket prices (Fare) correlated strongly with survival probability.
  • Family Dynamics: Small families (2-4 members) had optimal survival rates compared to solo travelers.
  • Port of Embarkation: Cherbourg passengers had highest survival rates (55% vs 34% overall).

Statistical Analysis Results

Gender Impact Analysis

Female passengers: 74% survival rate. Male passengers: 19% survival rate. The "women and children first" evacuation protocol clearly visible in data.

Class-Based Survival

1st Class: 63% survival, 2nd Class: 47% survival, 3rd Class: 24% survival. Clear socioeconomic hierarchy in disaster response.

Age Demographics

Children under 16 showed higher survival rates. Median age of survivors was 28 years, reflecting age-based evacuation priorities.

Family Size Effect

Optimal family size of 2-4 members. Solo travelers and very large families had lower survival probabilities.
